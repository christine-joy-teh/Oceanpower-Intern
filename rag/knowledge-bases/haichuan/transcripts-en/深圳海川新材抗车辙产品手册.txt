1 What problem are we facing?

Early-age distress of asphalt pavement on expressways has become a prominent issue affecting the healthy development of China's highways. The shortcomings mainly show in three ways:
First, distress appears early.
Second, the affected area is broad — premature pavement failure occurs to varying degrees across the country.
Third, the severity is high — among early pavement distresses, high-temperature rutting is the most prominent, appearing not only in the south but also severely in the north. This is mainly attributable to four factors:
1) Record-setting historic high temperatures.
2) Increasingly severe overloaded/heavy-load traffic.
3) The channelized traffic pattern of expressways and the unavoidable long steep grades of mountain expressways, which worsen rutting.
4) The "Specifications for Design of Highway Asphalt Pavement" (JTG D50-2006) requires a suspended-dense Type I waterproof structural layer, which creates a trade-off that worsens rutting.

2 What solutions do we have?

Three common approaches address rutting:
1. Gradation improvement — a double-edged sword: without expert handling it may improve rutting resistance while causing water damage.
2. Asphalt modification — generally effective but has compatibility issues between asphalt and the modifier; achieving higher rutting resistance requires high modifier dosage, which is hard to disperse evenly in the asphalt and can separate from the asphalt during transport and storage, sometimes performing worse than the base asphalt.

3 Why use Rad spunrie?

Rad spunrie is an asphalt-mixture additive composed of multiple polymers. Through multiple mechanisms — surface tackifying of the aggregate, reinforcement, filling, asphalt modification, and elastic recovery — it substantially improves the high-temperature stability of asphalt mixture, while also improving water-damage resistance and low-temperature cracking resistance. Compared with modified asphalt and traditional asphalt additives, Rad spunrie has clear advantages:

1) Superior performance: adding 0.3%-0.5% Rad spunrie to an ordinary asphalt mixture significantly improves high-temperature performance and improves resistance to water damage and low-temperature cracking.
2) High cost-effectiveness: unlike traditional asphalt modification, Rad spunrie is added directly into the mixing drum with the aggregate, requiring no special equipment, significantly lowering production energy consumption, and avoiding the storage-stability problems of modified asphalt.
3) Does not affect mix design: at any dosage it does not change the mixture gradation — only a minor adjustment to the oil-stone ratio is needed.
4) Simple construction process: added directly into the mixing drum, only requiring a slightly longer mixing time and higher construction temperature; mixing, paving and compaction are unaffected.
5) The direct-addition method allows dosage to match desired rutting resistance: unlike modified asphalt, the amount of Rad spunrie can be adjusted according to the desired degree of rutting resistance.

4 How does Rad spunrie work?

1) Cementing effect: during wet mixing and transport, Rad spunrie partially dissolves or swells in the asphalt, creating a cementing effect that raises softening point, increases viscosity, and reduces thermal sensitivity — achieving the effect of asphalt modification.
2) Reinforcing effect: the microcrystalline regions formed by the polymer have considerable stiffness; during mixing, part of it is drawn into plastic fibers that bridge and cross-link within the aggregate skeleton, forming a fiber-reinforcing effect.
3) Interlocking effect: Rad spunrie temporarily softens during construction, then these particles thermoform during compaction, acting like highly adhesive single-sized fine aggregate that fills voids in the aggregate skeleton, increasing the skeleton structure of the asphalt mixture while reducing the permeability of the finished pavement.
4) Deformation-recovery effect: the elastic component of Rad spunrie partially elastically recovers pavement deformation at relatively high temperatures, reducing permanent deformation of the finished asphalt pavement.

Application layer: depending on specific engineering conditions, analyze which structural layer mainly generates rutting to decide where to apply the composite polymer modifier. Generally it is applied to the upper and middle layers; in special cases it can be used in all three layers.
1) Type I (highest cost-performance): both middle and upper layers use the modifier — upper layer 3-4 cm fine-graded asphalt mixture + modifier; middle layer 5-6 cm medium-graded asphalt mixture + modifier; lower layer 6-8 cm coarse-graded asphalt mixture.
2) Type II (better performance): middle layer only — upper layer 4 cm fine-graded modified asphalt mixture/SMA; middle layer 5-6 cm medium-graded asphalt mixture + modifier; lower layer 6-8 cm coarse-graded asphalt mixture.
3) Type III (economical): upper layer only — upper layer 4 cm fine-graded ordinary asphalt mixture; middle layer 5-6 cm medium-graded asphalt mixture + modifier; lower layer 6-8 cm coarse-graded asphalt mixture.

Per European and American long-life pavement design philosophy, a long-life pavement is designed for a service life exceeding 40 years, with lower total routine maintenance cost than existing pavement structures. The basic long-life pavement structure model:
1) The 100-150 mm zone under the wheel load is a high-stress area, where most distress (mainly load-related) occurs.
2) The 40-75 mm surface layer of high-quality asphalt concrete provides a good driving surface for vehicles, with adequate surface texture depth, requiring rutting resistance and good water stability.
3) The 100-175 mm intermediate layer of high-modulus rutting-resistant asphalt concrete connects and distributes the load, requiring high modulus (stiffness) and rutting resistance.
4) The 75-100 mm HMA base layer of high-flexibility, fatigue-resistant asphalt concrete eliminates fatigue damage, requiring high flexibility, fatigue resistance, and good water stability.

European and American long-life pavement structure design requires that the entire surface structure have good rutting resistance, based on an accurate load/stress analysis of the whole pavement structure. Domestic practice, in contrast, often focuses only on the rutting resistance of the middle layer — a significant misconception. To achieve true durability and long life, the entire surface structure must have good rutting resistance.

Application method and dosage: the composite polymer modifier can replace the function of modified asphalt or fiber used alone, or be used together with modified asphalt and/or fiber. The typical dosage is 0.3%-0.5% of the asphalt mixture by weight, increasing to 0.7%-0.9% for special zones.

7 How to use Rad spunrie?

Design method:
Gradation: keep the gradation unchanged regardless of Rad spunrie dosage.
Optimum oil-stone ratio: at low dosage (0.3%-0.5%) keep the optimum oil-stone ratio unchanged. At high dosage (0.5%-0.7%), increase the optimum oil-stone ratio slightly by 0.1%-0.2%.
As with fiber, testing should account for the order of addition, extended dry-mixing time, and higher mixing temperature for Rad spunrie.

Construction process:
1. Mixing: cold aggregate of different specifications is fed into the drying drum at a set ratio; drying temperature is typically 180-190C. Under normal mixing conditions, a set proportion of Rad spunrie is added to the mixing drum with the aggregate for dry mixing, extending the original dry-mix time by 8-10 s, then hot asphalt (preheated to 160-170C) is sprayed in for wet mixing for 40 s, until uniformly mixed with no streaking (discharge temperature: 170-180C).
2. Transport: the mixed asphalt mixture is transported to the paving site by dump truck (ideally not less than 20 t).
3. Paving: preheat the paver's screed to about 120C, then discharge the mixture into the hopper for paving at about 2-3 m/min, with a loose-lay coefficient of about 1.15 and a paving temperature of 160-170C.
4. Compaction:
- For skeleton-dense mixtures, roll closely behind the paver using a "follow-close, roll-slow" technique.
- For suspended-dense mixtures, initial compaction temperature is 150-160C: static-roll once with a ~13 t steel-wheel roller, then 2 passes with a 26 t pneumatic-tire roller, 2 passes with a 13 t vibratory steel-wheel roller, and a final static pass with a 13 t steel-wheel roller, finishing no lower than 110-120C, until the required compaction is reached.

Scope of use:
1) Highways: expressways and high-grade highways, especially suited to hot climates, heavy-traffic sections, and long steep grades;
2) Municipal roads: main roads, bus lanes, etc., especially suited to intersections and bus stops;
3) Airport engineering: airport runways.

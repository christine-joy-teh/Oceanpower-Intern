Green Road — Haichuan EC Series Asphalt Concrete Warm-Mix Additives: The Best Choice for Energy-Saving, Emission-Reducing, and Low-Temperature Construction of Asphalt Concrete Pavement

Warm-mix asphalt mixture (WMA) is a category of new energy-saving, emission-reducing asphalt mixture whose mixing temperature falls between hot-mix asphalt mixture (HMA) and cold-mix asphalt mixture (CMA), while achieving performance equal to or better than HMA.

1 Product Introduction

EC-130 asphalt concrete warm-mix additive is a water-bearing inorganic mineral material (commonly known as zeolite); the water it contains is continuously released at around 100C, foaming the asphalt and reducing its viscosity, which lowers the mixing and construction temperature of asphalt concrete by 20-35C.

EC-120 asphalt concrete warm-mix modifier is a polyolefin-type viscosity-reducing modifier. Adding EC-120 changes the asphalt's viscosity-temperature curve and lowers its high-temperature viscosity, allowing the asphalt to mix evenly with aggregate at a relatively lower temperature.

Product advantages:
- Significantly lowers energy consumption in asphalt concrete production
- Reduces emissions of CO2 and other gases as well as dust
- Improves the construction environment
- Lower mixing temperature helps reduce asphalt aging during construction
- Extends the service life of asphalt concrete mixing equipment and lowers maintenance costs
- Warm-mix asphalt concrete pavement is easier to recycle later

2 Technical Analysis

1 Economical and energy-saving: compared with hot-mix asphalt concrete, warm-mix asphalt concrete lowers the mixing/construction temperature by about 30C. Producing 1 tonne of hot-mix asphalt concrete consumes about 8 L of fuel oil; using warm-mix technology saves 2-3 L of fuel per tonne.

2 Significant emission reduction: test data show that, compared with hot-mix, warm-mix asphalt concrete technology lowers the construction temperature by about 30C. With China producing about 280 million tonnes of asphalt concrete annually, fully switching to warm-mix technology could reduce CO2 emissions by about 756,000 tonnes per year and cut dust/smoke emissions by 75-90%.

Emissions comparison between hot-mix and warm-mix processes (test item / unit / HMA / WMA / reduction %):
- CO2: %: 2.6 / 1 / 61.5
- NOx: mg/m3: 151 / 40 / 73.5
- CO: mg/m3: 104 / 91.3 / 12.2
- SO2: 10,000 mg/m3: 13 / 3.3 / 74.6
- Smoke/dust: mg/m3: 5.6 / 2.59 / 53.8

On-site test results at the paving site from the National Environmental Analysis and Testing Center (test item / unit / hot-mix / warm-mix / reduction %):
- Asphalt fume: mg/m3: 21.1 / 2.06 / 90.2
- Benzene-soluble matter: mg/m3: 19.5 / 0.58 / 97.0
- Benzo[a]pyrene: mg/m3: 0.094 / 0.019 / 79.8

3 Good performance: per the "Standard Test Methods of Bitumen and Bituminous Mixtures for Highway Engineering" (JTG E20-2011), immersed Marshall testing and freeze-thaw split strength testing were used to evaluate water stability, and rutting tests were used to measure dynamic stability. Results show that warm-mix asphalt concrete with EC-series additives performs well, with all indicators comparable to or better than ordinary hot-mix asphalt concrete. Testing covered Marshall stability, split tensile strength and dynamic stability across different EC-additive dosages and gradations (AC13-1, AC13-2, AC16-1, AC16-2), with both EC-120 and EC-130 performing well.

3 Application Scope

Warm-mix technology can be applied anywhere hot-mix technology is used, and is especially suited to special situations and environments:
(1) Ultra-thin overlay — effectively solves compaction difficulties for ultra-thin overlays
(2) Long tunnel projects — reduces asphalt fume emissions and significantly improves the tunnel construction environment
(3) Low-temperature seasons, cold regions — effectively extends the construction season
(4) Centralized plant recycling of asphalt concrete — significantly improves recycling rates for old material
(5) Densely populated areas, urban roads — reduces the impact of pavement construction on nearby residents
